How to Get Turkish Citizenship by Property Investment

Turkey is one of the most popular choices for investors who want a second passport through real estate. The process is fairly simple, and with good advice it can move quickly. Below we explain how it works, what you need and what to expect.

Why consider Turkish citizenship by investment?

There are several practical reasons investors choose this route:

  1. Easy travel: A Turkish passport allows visa-free or visa-on-arrival access to over 110 countries.
  2. Dual citizenship: You don’t have to give up your current nationality.
  3. Strong investment environment: Turkey’s location, growing economy, and active property market make it attractive for investors.
  4. Your family is included: Your spouse and children under 18 can get citizenship too.
  5. No residency requirement: You don’t need to live in Turkey before or after applying.
  6. Quick timeline: Many applications are completed within 3 to 6 months.
  7. Public services: As a citizen you can use Turkey’s public healthcare and education.
  8. Investment potential: The property can earn rent and grow in value.

Basic requirements

To qualify through real estate investment, you’ll need to:

  • Invest at least $400,000 in property
  • Keep the property for at least three years
  • Register the purchase with the Land Registry
  • Transfer funds through a Turkish bank with proper records
  • Ensure the property has no legal issues or debts
  • Obtain an official valuation report
  • Choose any type of property (residential, commercial, land, or multiple units), as long as the total value meets the requirement

Step-by-step process

  1. Choose the right property
    • Work with a reliable real estate agent to find a property that meets the program criteria. Focus on areas with strong rental demand or growth potential.
  2. Do your due diligence
    • Hire a lawyer to confirm the property’s legal status and ownership. Open a Turkish bank account and review all documents carefully before signing anything.
  3. Complete the purchase
    • Transfer the funds through your Turkish bank account. Get an official valuation report, sign the sales contract, and register the title deed.
  4. Get a certificate of conformity
    • Apply for the certificate that confirms your investment meets the citizenship requirements. Make sure the title deed (TAPU) is correctly registered in your name.
  5. Apply for a residence permit
    • You’ll need a short-term residence permit while your citizenship application is being processed. This is usually issued within a few weeks.
  6. Apply for citizenship
    • Prepare your documents (passport, residence permit, proof of investment, photos, and tax number) and submit your application. In some cases, additional documents or an interview may be required.
  7. Receive approval
    • Once approved, you’ll receive Turkish citizenship along with your ID and passport. The full process typically takes a few months.

Things to keep in mind

  • Legal support matters: A good lawyer helps you avoid delays and costly mistakes.
  • Property isn’t the only route: If real estate isn’t for you, the program also accepts other investments, such as bank deposits or business investment.
  • Understand taxes: Be aware of property taxes, rental income tax, and possible capital gains tax.
  • Plan ahead: Think about your long-term goals: will you live in the property, rent it out, or sell it later?

Final thoughts

Buying property in Turkey can get you citizenship and a solid investment at the same time. The entry threshold is within reach for many investors, the process is clear, and the benefits go well beyond the passport.

If you’re considering this path, work with an experienced lawyer and property adviser; it makes the process smoother and helps you avoid expensive mistakes.
